# Deviations from docs/causal-model.md

Status: milestone 1 is a WIP. The demand side was redesigned (model doc section 8, items 1-9) and the game is turn-based. The model is steerable but not calibrated (see tuning-log.md). The `Status` column marks what the redesign changed.

| # | Deviation | Reason / symptom it was meant to fix | Status |
|---|---|---|---|

Balanced-budget variants were procyclical and spiralled into depression. Brake added because gov debt reached 4x GDP. | SUPERSEDED: removed with the stabiliser and debt brake. G is now the player's `public_spending` lever (dollars per month), no hidden reaction rule. |

## Demand levers (backlog item 2)
Verified through the existing plumbing, one lever at a time from the balanced start: public spending G (300k instead of 400k: unemployment 26%), benefit (1000 instead of 800: 4% in year 1), UBI (150: 0% in year 1, wages +40%, price index +15% over four years), wage tax (20%: same boom), min wage (1400: unemployment 17% and prices up, the sign test). So G is not the only lever; UBI and the wage tax are strong and show their cost as wage-price inflation and boom-bust cycles; the minimum wage lowers demand for labour. No new lever was added.

## Open problems
- Government debt/GDP drifts from 0.4 to about 1.1 over 40 years in baseline A (1.35 in B, which borrows for plants and housing). The deficit is the private sector's net saving, so austerity makes it worse (raising wage and capital taxes in B ended in legitimacy 0).
- Extraction (C) is not punished by the economy itself: collapsing the commons or cutting taxes does not lower wellbeing or output much. C fails only through the carbon budget and a boom-bust wage-price cycle.
- Fossil plants have no emission effect (item 46).
- Tax and benefit changes are very expansionary when unemployment is low: wage-price spirals (prices x2 in C).
- The wealth tax is inert at the default threshold (500,000 per person; the richest cohort holds about 50,000 per person): the player must lower `wealth_threshold`.
